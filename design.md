# Design.md --- Portfolio Visual Design System

## 1. Core Design Concept

The portfolio is based on a **painterly editorial aesthetic** derived
from the supplied portrait and botanical references.

The visual language should feel like a carefully composed art book
translated into a modern digital portfolio.

The key combination is:

**Painterly portrait + realistic watercolor flowers + editorial
typography + modern web layout + restrained UI.**

The portfolio should not look like a generic SaaS portfolio or a generic
watercolor template.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Primary Visual Language

There are now two complementary visual worlds:

### A. Portrait painting

The portrait uses: - Stronger contrast - Expressive brushwork - Defined
facial planes - Pink/magenta skin treatment - Deep plum shadows -
Olive-green clothing - More deliberate directional strokes

### B. Botanical watercolor

The flowers should use: - Realistic botanical structure - Transparent
watercolor washes - Soft pigment transitions - Visible paper-like
texture - Delicate edges - Layered translucent petals - Natural
variation in color density - Small pigment blooms - Fine botanical
details

The flowers should feel **hand-painted with real watercolor**, not like
flat digital clip-art.

The portrait and flowers can therefore have different levels of detail
while sharing the same handmade visual language.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Composition

### Overall composition

Use a strong visual hierarchy:

1.  Face / portrait
2.  Name and role
3.  Primary statement
4.  Selected work
5.  Botanical visual moments
6.  About / capabilities
7.  Experience
8.  Contact

The portrait should remain the primary identity element.

Flowers should support the composition rather than replace the portrait.

### Asymmetry

Do not force every section into a symmetrical grid.

Use: - Offset text - Large empty spaces - Portrait overlap - Cropped
botanical elements - Watercolor blooms extending beyond containers -
Uneven visual weight

The asymmetry should feel intentional rather than chaotic.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Portrait Treatment

The portrait is a **cutout illustration**, not a standard image.

Characteristics: - Head and upper chest are visible. - Face is the
dominant area. - Small amount of neck/chest is included. - Background is
transparent. - Skin uses pink and cream tones. - Hair uses deep plum and
magenta. - Clothing uses olive green.

### Placement

The hero portrait can: - Extend beyond the hero container - Overlap a
section boundary - Sit partially behind typography - Be cropped slightly
at the bottom

Do not crop through the eyes or important facial features.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Brushwork

## Brush stroke character

The portrait brushwork should look energetic but controlled.

Use: - Long directional strokes - Short broken strokes - Dry-brush
edges - Layered strokes - Sharp painterly cuts - Small textured marks -
Uneven pigment edges

Avoid making the portrait look like smooth vector artwork.

### Stroke direction

Strokes should follow the subject.

Hair: - Curved directional strokes following hair flow

Face: - Smaller strokes following facial planes

Neck: - Longer diagonal strokes

Clothing: - Broader directional strokes following fabric structure

Decorative background: - Large sweeping strokes

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Paint Layering

Build the portrait appearance in layers:

### Layer 1 --- Base

Flat underlying color.

### Layer 2 --- Midtone

Primary local color.

### Layer 3 --- Shadow

Deep magenta, plum, olive, or dark neutral.

### Layer 4 --- Light

Cream, pale pink, or warm white.

### Layer 5 --- Accent

Small high-saturation strokes.

### Layer 6 --- Texture

Dry-brush and broken pigment marks.

The texture should be irregular rather than evenly distributed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Lighting

Use strong directional lighting for the portrait.

The portrait should have: - Bright cream/pale-pink planes - Pink
midtones - Deep wine/magenta shadow areas - Small near-white highlights

Highlights should be concentrated on: - Forehead - Nose bridge - Nose
tip - Upper cheek - Lips - Chin - Hair edges

Do not illuminate every surface equally.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Face Rendering

The face should remain recognizable and structured.

Use painterly planes rather than photographic gradients.

### Skin

Base: - Soft warm pink

Midtones: - Blush pink - Rose - Coral-pink

Shadows: - Deep raspberry - Wine - Plum

Highlights: - Pale blush - Cream - Warm white

### Facial planes

Use darker strokes under: - Brow - Nose - Cheekbone - Lower lip - Jaw -
Chin - Beard area

Use lighter strokes on: - Forehead - Nose ridge - Cheek - Upper lip
area - Chin

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Hair

Hair should be represented with many directional strokes.

Base: - Very dark plum / near-black

Secondary: - Deep magenta

Highlights: - Hot pink - Rose pink - Pale pink

The highlights should follow the natural direction of the hair instead
of appearing as random lines.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Eyes

Eyes are a high-priority identity feature.

Keep: - Slightly hooded shape - Natural asymmetry - Dark brown irises -
Subtle lower-eye shading - Realistic glasses interaction - Small
controlled highlights

Do not exaggerate the eyes into an anime or cartoon style.

The eyes should remain realistic enough to preserve likeness while
fitting the painterly style.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Glasses

Glasses should be: - Dark rectangular frames - Clearly defined -
Slightly reflective - Integrated into the brushwork

Reflections should be subtle.

Do not make the glasses glow.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Clothing

The clothing is an olive-green Cuban-collar shirt.

Use: - Olive base - Moss-green midtone - Dark olive shadow - Pale
yellow-green highlights

The collar should be clearly visible.

Use broad fabric-following brush strokes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. Botanical / Flower Direction

The flower imagery should be based on the three supplied watercolor
references.

The flowers should look like **realistic hand-painted watercolor
botanical illustrations**, with the softness and transparency of
watercolor on paper.

Do not make them look like: - Flat vector flowers - 3D-rendered
flowers - Plastic digital flowers - Generic AI floral blobs - Hard-edged
cartoon flowers

The goal is **natural botanical realism interpreted through
watercolor**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14. Flower Structure

The flowers should have believable botanical construction.

Preserve: - Individual petals - Petal overlap - Natural petal
curvature - Central reproductive structures - Stamens - Pistils - Sepals
where visible - Stems - Leaves - Buds

Each petal should have its own shape rather than being represented by a
single generic radial form.

### Petal arrangement

Petals should overlap naturally.

Use: - Larger outer petals - Smaller inner petals - Irregular spacing -
Slight asymmetry - Natural folding - Curved edges

Avoid perfect geometric symmetry.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15. Watercolor Petal Rendering

This is one of the most important visual rules.

Each petal should contain several translucent watercolor layers.

### Base wash

Start with a very light, transparent wash.

### Midtone wash

Add pigment toward the inner or overlapping regions.

### Shadow wash

Use deeper color where: - Petals overlap - Petals fold inward - Petals
connect to the center

### Highlight

Leave some paper/light areas visible.

Do not fill every part of the petal with opaque color.

The paper should visually participate in the flower.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16. Watercolor Transparency

The flowers must retain watercolor transparency.

Use: - Low-opacity pigment - Layered washes - Uneven pigment density -
Soft transitions - Transparent overlaps

A petal should sometimes allow the underlying wash to visually influence
its color.

Avoid perfectly uniform fills.

The result should feel like pigment has soaked into paper.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 17. Watercolor Edges

Edges should vary.

### Soft edges

Use for: - Outer petals - Background washes - Faded leaves - Distant
floral elements

### Controlled edges

Use for: - Petal overlaps - Flower centers - Important silhouettes -
Stamens

### Broken edges

Use occasionally where: - Pigment runs out - The brush lifts - Water
causes uneven deposition

Do not outline every petal with a hard digital stroke.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 18. Watercolor Pigment Behaviour

Introduce subtle natural watercolor behaviour:

-   Pigment pooling near some edges
-   Slight granulation
-   Uneven wash density
-   Small blooms
-   Soft backruns
-   Tiny splatters
-   Feathered transitions
-   Dry-brush marks in selected areas

These effects should be subtle.

Do not cover the entire flower with random splatter.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 19. Flower Color Behaviour

The pink flowers should use a family of related watercolor pigments
rather than one flat pink.

Suggested visual progression:

**Warm white → pale blush → soft rose → coral pink → deeper
rose/crimson**

The deepest pink should generally occur: - Toward petal bases - In
overlapping folds - Around the flower center - In selected shadow
pockets

The outer petal edges should often be lighter and more transparent.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 20. White / Pale Flower Areas

White flowers should not be pure white shapes.

Represent them with: - Warm paper white - Very pale pink - Very pale
grey-green - Soft cool shadows

The white areas should be created partly by **leaving the paper
visible**.

This is important to achieve a convincing watercolor appearance.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 21. Flower Centers

Flower centers need more detail than the outer petals.

Use: - Fine brush marks - Small stamens - Yellow/gold accents where
appropriate - Darker central shadows - Small irregular pigment marks

The center should provide a visual focal point.

Do not make it look like a perfectly symmetrical icon.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 22. Leaves

Leaves should have the same watercolor logic as the flowers.

Use: - Desaturated sage - Olive green - Deep forest green - Blue-green
accents

Leaves should contain: - A visible central vein - Soft secondary veins -
Transparent washes - Uneven pigment - Natural curvature

Avoid perfectly flat green leaf shapes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 23. Stems

Stems should be delicate and slightly imperfect.

Use: - Thin watercolor strokes - Darker pigment around joints - Slight
variations in thickness - Soft fading toward the ends

Do not use perfectly straight vector lines.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 24. Buds and Secondary Flowers

Small buds can be used as supporting visual elements.

They should: - Be smaller than the hero flower - Have less detail - Use
lighter washes - Create a natural visual rhythm

This can help create a botanical composition without overcrowding the
page.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 25. Botanical Background Washes

The supplied references use loose watercolor washes around the flowers.

Use: - Very pale pink - Warm white - Soft sage - Faded blue-green where
appropriate - Extremely low-opacity pigment

These washes should feel accidental and organic.

Use irregular shapes rather than perfect gradients.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 26. Watercolor Splatter

Small splatters can be used as a finishing layer.

Good: - Tiny pigment dots - A few larger irregular drops - Sparse marks
around flowers - Occasional pigment specks near the composition

Avoid: - Uniform dotted patterns - Excessive splatter - Splatter behind
every component

Splatter should feel like a consequence of painting, not a UI
decoration.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 27. Gold / Metallic Accents

The references include occasional warm gold marks.

Use them very sparingly.

Good uses: - Tiny floral accents - A few paint flecks - Small decorative
details

The gold should look like irregular metallic watercolor or paint rather
than a digital gradient.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 28. Botanical Composition

Flowers can appear as:

### Hero botanical

One large flower or small cluster.

### Corner botanical

Flowers partially entering from a corner.

### Floating botanical

A small flower/bud separated from the main composition.

### Border botanical

A loose branch or stem crossing the edge of a section.

Avoid placing large floral clusters everywhere.

One strong botanical moment is better than many competing decorations.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 29. Relationship Between Portrait and Flowers

The portrait and flowers should feel like they belong to the same visual
world.

The distinction should remain:

**Portrait = expressive painted illustration**

**Flowers = delicate realistic watercolor**

They should share: - Handmade quality - Organic edges - Natural pigment
variation - Pink/rose accents - Green supporting tones - Cream
paper-like space

Do not force the flowers into the exact same heavy brush style as the
portrait.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 30. Paper Texture

For watercolor areas, use a subtle paper texture.

The texture should be: - Fine - Low contrast - Organic - Slightly
irregular

It should be visible mainly when looking closely.

Avoid obvious paper grain that makes the website look noisy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 31. UI Design

The interface should be cleaner than the artwork.

The portrait and flowers contain visual complexity; the UI provides
structure.

Use: - Thin borders - Minimal cards - Moderate corner radius - Editorial
spacing - Strong typography - Small iconography - Limited shadows

The UI should not compete with the illustration.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 32. Typography

Use a modern editorial type system.

Recommended pairing:

Display: - Instrument Serif - Playfair Display - Cormorant Garamond

UI / Body: - Inter - Manrope - Geist

Use large display typography for: - Hero statement - Section headings -
Project titles

Use clean sans-serif typography for: - Navigation - Metadata - Buttons -
Body copy

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 33. Borders and Dividers

Use thin, slightly imperfect-looking dividers where appropriate.

Possible treatments: - 1px neutral border - Broken brush line - Short
painted stroke - Small dot + line combination

Do not make every section look boxed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 34. Buttons

Buttons should be simple and editorial.

Primary: - Dark plum background - Cream text

Secondary: - Transparent - Thin border - Dark plum text

Hover: - Slight translation - Small underline/brush reveal - No
exaggerated scaling

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 35. Cards

Project cards should feel like art boards rather than SaaS cards.

Use: - Large image - Small metadata - Large title - Short description -
Minimal border

Avoid excessive card shadows.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 36. Decorative Elements

Use a limited set of recurring visual motifs:

-   Brush strokes
-   Watercolor washes
-   Small paint splatters
-   Fine botanical stems
-   Small dots
-   Cropped color blocks
-   Underlines
-   Irregular masks

Repeat these motifs consistently.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 37. Motion

Motion should mimic paint and editorial transitions.

Good: - Stroke drawing - Watercolor wash reveal - Mask reveal - Fade +
slight translation - Image reveal - Text slide by a few pixels - Very
subtle botanical drift

Timing: - Micro interaction: 150--250ms - Standard transition:
300--500ms - Hero reveal: 600--900ms

Use easing that feels smooth and deliberate.

Do not animate flowers as if they are 3D objects.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 38. Design Principle

The strongest visual rule is:

> The interface should frame the artwork, not compete with it.

The portrait is the personality. The flowers are the delicate organic
counterpoint. Typography is the voice. Brushwork is the texture.
Watercolor is the softness. The UI is the structure.

The final website should feel like **a designer's digital canvas that
combines expressive portrait painting with delicate botanical
watercolor**.
